About

A polymer knowledge base that improves with every question

Ask a question about polymers and a research agent answers it, reading what this project already knows before it searches open sources. Everything it reads is saved with its source and a label saying who has checked it, and the next question reads it first. Each answer leaves the knowledge base larger, and better checked, than it found it.

Ask a questionHow the loop works

01

The loop

Five steps turn every answer into knowledge for the next one.

  1. Ask. You type a question and choose a depth: how much time and searching the answer may use.
  2. Answer. The research agent first sends a query to saved knowledge, and turns to web search only for what that does not cover. Its answer gives a citation for everything a source reports, and labels its own reasoning Interpretation and textbook knowledge Background.
  3. Save. Each value it read is saved as a finding, with its source, its place in that source and its conditions. Each document it opened gets a source note.
  4. Review. A separate reviewer, a fresh session of the same AI engine, opens the sources behind the findings the agent read off a figure, converted or combined, and behind a sample of the rest, and labels each one.
  5. Reuse. The next question's knowledge search finds those findings first.

Around the loop

It asks itself

Each answer ends with a few questions it could not settle. With background research switched on (it is off by default), they join a research queue that the loop works through on its own, a few questions a week.

It is measured

On demand, a fixed parity set of eight questions is answered by the research agent and by a plain session, and one checker compares the two. When people change the agent's instructions, this shows whether its answers got better or worse.

02

What accumulates

The first question about a polymer reads mostly from the web. Its findings stay attached to that polymer, so the next question starts from them and searches only for what is missing, while reviews turn added findings into reviewed ones, or reject them. The knowledge base is cumulative: over time, answers rest more on checked, cited knowledge and less on fresh searching.

03

What a finding holds

A number means little without its context. Polylactide is a polymer family; NatureWorks 3100HP is one material, or grade; a moulded bar is the specimen actually measured; and its glass transition temperature shifts with the conditions, here a DSC scan at 10 °C per minute. A finding keeps all of these, with the exact place in the source, so a reader can judge where the value applies and check it.

04

Who vouches for a fact

Every finding keeps its provenance: where it was read, and who has checked it. One label says which, in the same colours the app uses.

Reviewed
A separate reviewer opened the source and found that it supports the finding.
Added
Read directly in the source and saved on the agent's own judgement. Used, but not yet checked by anyone else.
Needs checking
Read off a figure, converted or combined and not yet reviewed, or the reviewer could not confirm it. Treat it as a lead.
Rejected
The source does not support it. Hidden from searches, kept with the reason.
What “Reviewed” does not mean. The reviewer confirmed that the source says what the finding says. It did not repeat the experiment, and a published value can still be wrong.

05

The graph underneath

An ontology is an agreed vocabulary for a subject: the kinds of things it describes and how they may be related, written precisely enough for a computer to use. Here the facts are stored as a knowledge graph. Each thing is a node with a permanent identifier, and each relationship is an edge whose label, the predicate, says how two nodes relate. Every edge is itself a claim with its own evidence and status, so of any link, not only any node, you can ask who says so.

The graph began as that seed: 274 polymer families, 245 chemical structures and 27,559 records, each labelled an imported draft, because importing a sourced statement is not the same as checking it. Saved findings attach to its polymer families.

06

Keeping it honest

Privacy. Your question, and the saved findings relevant to it, go to the engine's provider (OpenAI or Anthropic) while it is answered and reviewed. The knowledge base itself is stored on this computer, and your questions, the agents' working records and saved excerpts never enter a public release.

Limits. AI agents misread tables and mislabel units. The labels exist because of this: they say how much checking each fact has had, so you can decide how far to rely on it. Matching a plain session on the parity set is a guardrail, not the goal; the project's value is provenance and memory.

Origins. The seed comes from the Wiki of Open Polymer Data. Original prose and diagrams here are licensed CC BY 4.0, structured data CC0 and the code Apache 2.0. Linked sources keep their own rights: the project links to their full text rather than copying it.

Reference

Glossary

Every term on this page, in one place

The knowledge base

Ontology

An agreed vocabulary for a subject: the kinds of things it describes and the ways they may be related, written precisely enough for a computer to use. Think of a field's dictionary and grammar together.

In this project It says that a material can be a materialOf a polymer family, and that a measurement must state its conditions.

Related: knowledge graph, predicate, claim

Knowledge graph

Facts stored as a network rather than a table: things are nodes and the relationships between them are edges, so you can follow connections from one fact to the next.

In this project The seed's 274 polymer families and 27,559 records, plus every saved finding.

Related: node, edge, seed

Node

One thing in a graph, with its own record and a permanent identifier: a polymer family, a structure, a source.

In this project The polylactide family, wop-000217.

Related: edge, identifier

Edge

A labelled link between two nodes that says how they relate. Here every edge is also a claim, with its own evidence and status.

In this project Poly(L-lactide) → broader → polylactide family.

Related: predicate, claim, node

Predicate

The label on an edge: the verb of the relationship, chosen from the ontology's fixed list.

In this project narrower: a more specific member of the family; hasStructure: drawn with this repeat unit; hasFacet: carries this tag; hasProcess: made by this route; evidence: the source behind a claim.

Related: edge, ontology

Identifier

A permanent name that a computer can rely on, even when the names people use vary.

In this project PLA, polylactide and poly(lactic acid) all lead to wop-000217.

Related: node

Claim

A single assertion that can be checked: a subject, a predicate and an object, together with its evidence and its status.

In this project “The polylactide family hasStructure the poly-L-lactide unit”, with ChEBI entry 53408 as its evidence.

Related: edge, provenance, imported draft

Polymer family

A group of polymers that share a chemistry or repeat unit. A family is an idea, not a product you can buy.

In this project The polylactide family, which includes poly(L-lactide).

Related: material, specimen

Material

A particular product or grade of a polymer. Grades of one family can differ in molecular weight, additives and stereochemistry, and so in their properties.

In this project NatureWorks 3100HP, a commercial PLA grade.

Related: polymer family, specimen

Specimen

The physical piece that was actually measured, such as a moulded bar, a film or a printed disc. How it was made affects what is measured.

In this project An injection-moulded bar, made with the mould at 110 °C.

Related: material, conditions

Repeat unit

The small chemical unit that repeats along a polymer chain, drawn in square brackets with a subscript n.

In this project The lactide unit in Figure 6, drawn from the seed's own structure file.

Related: polymer family

Facet

A tag that sorts polymers along one axis, such as origin or behaviour. A polymer can carry many.

In this project Polylactide is bio based, biodegradable and thermoplastic.

Related: node, predicate

Seed

The starting content of the knowledge graph, imported from the Wiki of Open Polymer Data.

In this project 274 polymer families, 245 structures and 27,559 records.

Related: imported draft, knowledge graph

Imported draft

The label for seed content: it came with sources, but this project has not checked it.

In this project Every claim in the seed.

Related: seed, reviewed

Evidence

Finding

One value read from one source, saved with everything needed to check it: the material, the property, the value and unit, the conditions, and where in the source it appears.

In this project A glass transition of 58.5 °C for one PLA grade, from Table 3 of an open-access article (Figure 3).

Related: source, locator, conditions

Source

The document a fact comes from: a journal article, handbook, standard, datasheet or reference database.

In this project An open-access article in Polymers, or the ChEBI chemical database.

Related: citation, locator

Citation

A pointer from a statement to the finding or source that supports it, so a reader can go and check.

In this project The small numbered link after a sentence in an answer.

Related: source, finding

Locator

Where in a source a value appears, precisely enough to find it again.

In this project “Table 3, PLA 60 s row”.

Related: excerpt, source

Excerpt

A short verbatim quote, under about 25 words, that shows a value with its label. It is kept privately so the value can be checked, and is not republished.

In this project Shown as a striped bar in Figure 3.

Related: locator, reviewer

Conditions

How a value was measured: the method, temperature, rate and sample preparation. A glass transition temperature without its heating rate is half a fact.

In this project DSC, first heating scan at 10 °C/min, under nitrogen.

Related: specimen, DSC

Glass transition temperature

The temperature range over which the amorphous part of a polymer changes from hard and glassy to soft and rubbery, written Tg. The measured value depends on the method and the heating rate.

In this project About 58 °C for the PLA grade in Figure 3.

Related: DSC, conditions

DSC

Differential scanning calorimetry. A sample and an empty reference pan are heated at a set rate, and the difference in heat flow shows where the polymer softens, crystallises or melts.

In this project The values in Figure 3 were measured this way.

Related: glass transition temperature, conditions

Provenance

The record of where a fact came from and who has checked it: a chain of custody for information.

In this project Every finding's source, locator and label.

Related: citation, reviewed, claim

Source note

A short record of a source the research agent read, including one that turned out not to contain what it was looking for.

In this project It saves a later question from reading the same document for nothing.

Related: source, finding

The loop

Question

What you ask, in plain language. One question usually leads to several queries.

In this project “How does annealing affect the crystallinity of PLA films?”

Related: query, depth

Query

A request to search stored information. The research agent turns your question into queries.

In this project A knowledge search for “PLA glass transition”.

Related: knowledge search, question

Research agent

An AI model working to fixed instructions. It searches saved knowledge and the web, reads sources, writes the answer and saves what it found.

In this project It runs on Codex or Claude Code.

Related: engine, reviewer

Engine

The AI service that runs the research agent and the reviewer, through your own subscription.

In this project OpenAI's Codex or Anthropic's Claude Code.

Related: research agent

Depth

How much time and searching an answer may use.

In this project Quick (5 minutes), Standard (10) or Thorough (20).

Related: question, web search

Knowledge search

The research agent's first step: finding the saved findings most relevant to the question.

In this project Results come back grouped: reviewed, then added, then needing checking.

Related: query, cumulative

Web search

Searching open sources online. The research agent uses it only for what saved knowledge does not cover.

In this project About ten searches at Standard depth.

Related: source, depth

Interpretation

A statement that is the research agent's own reasoning, such as a comparison or a calculation. It is labelled, cites its premises, and is never attributed to a paper.

In this project “Interpretation: the two values differ because the heating rates differ.”

Related: background, citation

Background

Textbook knowledge in an answer, labelled as such and given without a citation.

In this project “Background: PLA is an aliphatic polyester.”

Related: interpretation

Reviewer

A separate AI session that opens a finding's cited source and decides whether the source supports it. It did not write the answer: it runs in a fresh session of the engine that did, which can open the sources that engine read.

In this project It checks figure readings, conversions and combined values, and a sample of added ones, and returns supported, unsupported or uncertain with one sentence of reason.

Related: reviewed, spot check

Reviewed

A reviewer found that the source supports the finding.

In this project Shown with a green badge.

Related: reviewer, added

Added

Saved on the research agent's own judgement, because it read the value directly in the source. Later questions use it, but no one else has checked it.

In this project Shown with a grey badge. One in five is spot-checked.

Related: reviewed, spot check

Needs checking

Waiting for a reviewer, or a reviewer could not confirm it. Treat it as a lead, not a fact.

In this project Shown with an amber badge.

Related: reviewer, rejected

Rejected

A reviewer found that the source does not support the finding. It is hidden from searches but kept, with the reason.

In this project Shown with a red badge.

Related: reviewer, needs checking

Spot check

A random sample of added findings that the reviewer also checks, to measure how often the research agent's judgement holds.

In this project One in five by default; adjustable in Settings.

Related: added, reviewer

Cumulative

Building up over time: each answer leaves the knowledge base better supplied for the next.

In this project Figure 2 shows the mechanism.

Related: knowledge search, finding

Research queue

Questions waiting to be researched in the background: the open questions from your answers, once background research is switched on. The loop asks them itself, within a weekly limit.

In this project Off by default; set how many questions a week in Settings.

Related: question, cumulative

Parity set

Eight fixed questions, answered by a plain session and by the research agent and judged by one checker, to track quality and cost.

In this project Run on demand, for example after a change to the research agent's instructions.

Related: plain session, guardrail

Plain session

An AI with web search but none of this project's saved knowledge or instructions: the point of comparison.

In this project The same engine, asked the same question.

Related: parity set

Guardrail

A limit that warns when something gets worse, rather than a target to optimise.

In this project At most one more statement contradicted by its source and one fewer complete answer than the plain session, at no more than about one and a half times its cost.

Related: parity set, plain session
